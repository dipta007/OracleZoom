Abstract

Recursive super-resolution reaches extreme magnification by applying a 4× diffusion backbone step after step. At these factors the backbone cannot recover detail, it must invent it. So the question is not whether the zoom looks sharp, but whether the invented detail is faithful or hallucinated, and methods tuned for sharpness hallucinate more as depth grows.

We teach faithfulness by privileged-latent distillation: a teacher allowed to see the ground-truth high-resolution patch at training time only distills its real detail into the student, in decode space. Because ground truth exists only at 4×, we carry this faithful prior deeper by letting the student pursue a detail reward, and leash that reward with a KL term to the trusted base model, so the added detail stays inside the distilled prior instead of drifting into a metric-gaming texture.

Our zoom is as sharp as six recent methods on no-reference metrics, and far more faithful: most faithful at 4× across the benchmark, and past 4× two cross-family vision–language judges prefer it. All of this comes from a 7.1M-parameter rank-16 adapter on a frozen backbone, trained on only 1,000 curated images, and it drops into the existing recursion unchanged.

The problem: a sharpness score cannot tell truth from invention

Past 4× there is no ground truth to compare against. The usual response is to optimise a no-reference sharpness metric. But such a metric rewards confident detail, not correct detail, so it can be gamed.

Our own ablation shows this directly. Remove the KL leash and the model produces the sharpest scores of any configuration (CLIPIQA 0.756, the best cell in the study) while also producing the most hallucination (0.365, the worst cell). Sharper and less true at the same time. The reward stops adding real detail and starts printing a repetitive crosshatch.

Unleashed, the sharpness reward games the metric with a repetitive texture. The KL leash is what keeps the added detail inside the distilled prior.

The objective

Five terms. Weights are the reported configuration. x̂1 is the 4× output, x̂2 the 16× output, z a latent.

A · 4× fidelity  LPIPS(x̂1, x1)  ×1.0

Match the real high-resolution patch. This is the only scale where a true answer exists, and it is measured in picture space, not latent space: an ablation found a latent target was worse than doing no distillation at all.

B · Cycle coherence  LPIPS(↓4x̂2, Π(x1))  ×1.0

Zoom to 16×, shrink the result back by 4, and check it still matches the region it zoomed into. A consistency rule replaces a label that does not exist.

C · Sharpness reward  −TOPIQ-NR(x̂2)  ×0.4

Coherence alone rewards playing safe, because shrinking a blurry image also matches. Something has to actively ask for detail past 4×.

D · KL leash  ‖z2 − z2base‖²  ×8.0

Penalise drift from the untouched base model on the same input. The largest weight of the five, because it restrains the one term that misbehaves. The base is fixed, never a moving average: a moving anchor would anchor nothing.

E · Privileged EMA  ‖z1 − z1EMA‖²  ×0.1

A slow average copy of the adapter, shown the privileged input, gives a second and steadier 4× target. The smallest term, and honestly the least load-bearing: removing it moves every metric within noise.

Faithfulness past 4×

Where no reference exists, we ask two vision–language judges from model families outside our pipeline, so the evaluation is not self-referential. Each item is region-aligned and order-randomised, with labels burned into the images so the judge must read the label rather than infer identity from ordering. Replies that do not parse are recorded as abstentions, never guessed, and win-rate is computed over decided items only.

Both judges prefer our zoom. The main judge prefers it 68–78% of the time at 64–256×, and finds 2–5× more hallucination in Chain-of-Zoom, the strongest baseline. At 4× the test does not separate the two (win-rate 0.50, interval [0.38, 0.63]); the full-reference metrics settle that scale.

Qualitative comparison

At 64× and 256×. Regression backbones go smooth, other diffusion methods invent texture that drifts from the scene. Ours keeps adding detail that stays consistent with what the previous zoom level actually showed.

The full ladder: 4× → 16× → 64× → 256×. Each step crops the centre and zooms again, so cost per step is flat across scales.

python -m opd_zoom.teacher.oracle_infer --mode student \
  --pld_lora path/to/adapter \
  --gt_dir my_images/ --out out/ --rec_num 4 --upscale 4
# out/scale1 = 4x  ...  out/scale4 = 256x
